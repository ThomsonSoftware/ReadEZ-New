Welcome to ReadEZ

ReadEZ screens for visual stress (pattern-related visual discomfort) and finds the colour that helps a patient most: the colour of a ReadEZ overlay or reading guide, of ReadEZ tinted lenses, or of a computer screen.

The aim of the screening is:

  • to identify those individuals who are likely to benefit from the use of colour, and
  • to determine the optimum colour for ReadEZ overlays, ReadEZ lenses and computer screens.

The tests

  1. Baseline symptoms without colour
  2. Baseline rate of reading without colour (the Wilkins Rate of Reading test)
  3. Determination of the preferred colour: automatic or manual, for overlays, lenses or a computer screen
  4. Rate of reading with the preferred colour
  5. Symptoms with the preferred colour

See A step-by-step guide.

Getting started

Before testing patients:

  1. Install and activate ReadEZ.
  2. If you used ReadEZ 2020, import its patients and results.
  3. Set up the screens (one or two) in Settings > Screens.
  4. Check the ReadEZ colours on the screen the patient will use.
  5. Prepare the test room.

Installing and activating

Run ReadEZ-Setup.msi. ReadEZ is installed in Program Files\Thomson Software Solutions\ReadEZ and can be installed alongside ReadEZ 2020.

The first time ReadEZ starts it asks for your licence. If ReadEZ 2020 was activated on this computer its licence is taken over automatically. Otherwise enter the serial number (it starts RZ-) and activate online, or use the offline activation code from Thomson Software Solutions.

ReadEZ checks for updates when it starts and from Help > Check for updates. An update keeps your settings, licence, patients, tests and reading texts.

Setting up the screens

ReadEZ uses two windows: the control window (what you work in) and the patient's screen (what the patient looks at). In Settings > Screens choose where each goes.

The test environment

The test should be administered in a quiet room, free from distractions. The room lighting should be subdued and care should be taken to avoid reflections on the screen.

The patient should be seated comfortably in front of the screen with the examiner seated to one side. The screen should be viewed from approximately 40 cm and the patient should wear the spectacles that they normally use for reading (if any).

The lenses test is done in a darkened room so that the whole visual field is coloured; the computer test with the room lights on.

Before screening, confirm that the patient has had a recent eye examination by an optometrist, to rule out an ocular cause of the symptoms.

Importing from ReadEZ 2020

ReadEZ keeps its data in a new database (Documents\ReadEZ Data\Database\ReadEZ.db). Patients, organisations, screeners, baseline tests and colour results from ReadEZ 2020 (ReadEZ2020.db) can be imported:

  • automatically: the first time ReadEZ starts, it offers to import the ReadEZ 2020 database it finds;
  • at any time: File > Import a ReadEZ 2020 database, or Settings > Data.

The old database is only read, never changed. A backup is made before importing. Importing the same database again adds only patients and tests that are new, so nothing is duplicated.

ReadEZ 2020's settings (text, timings, screens and its palette) can be imported in Settings > Admin > Maintenance from RZSettings.ini. After importing, check the ReadEZ colours again.

Patients

Search by name, reference or date of birth, and filter by organisation (school, practice …). Select a patient to see their details and tests.

  • New patient: title, names, date of birth, reference, contact details and organisation.

Colour calibration

ReadEZ may be used to determine the optimum colour for ReadEZ overlays / reading guides, ReadEZ tinted lenses and computer screens. In each case it is very important that the colours displayed on the screen match the overlays and lenses. Displays reproduce colour differently, so check the colours on the screen the patient uses before testing (with two screens, on the patient's screen).

The Colours page shows the three sets – overlays, light lens tints and dark lens tints, each with 12 colours (A – L) and grey – with a tick and date for each colour checked on this screen.

Checking a colour

  1. Click Check colours (or a single colour).
  2. Hold the overlay on white paper (or the lens) next to the colour shown on the patient's screen.
  3. If they match, confirm. If not, choose the closest of the colours offered; a few choices find the match.

The top bar shows how many colours have been checked; the Tests page warns if some have not.

Running a test

Select a patient, then choose a test on the Tests page:

TestWhat it does
BaselineSymptoms and rate of reading on a white background.
OverlaysThe colour for an overlay or reading guide: two passages side by side, each surrounded by an overlay colour.
LensesThe colour for tinted lenses: the whole screen coloured, one colour after the other, in a darkened room.
ComputerThe colour for a computer screen: as Lenses, with the room lit.

Choose the colour test method (automatic or manual) and whether rate of reading and symptoms with the colour follow straight on. Tests can be switched off in Settings > Admin.

A step-by-step guide

  1. Baseline: symptoms without colour, then rate of reading without colour.
  2. Colour test for overlays, lenses or computer: find the preferred colour.
  3. Rate of reading with the preferred colour.
  4. Symptoms with the preferred colour.

The Results page then compares the colour test with the baseline. An increase in reading speed of 5% or more, fewer errors and fewer symptoms suggest the colour helps. If grey was preferred to the chosen colour, colour may be of little benefit.

The program is a screening tool. Results should be interpreted together with the patient's history and an eye examination.

One or two screens

The test screen shows the steps of the test along the top, the controls for the current step, and a view of the patient's screen.

  • Two screens: the patient's screen is on its own monitor and stays in front while the test runs; the view in the test screen is an exact copy, so clicks on it (marking errors, choosing colours) act on what the patient sees.
  • One screen: the patient reads from the view in the test screen. Press F11 (or Patient full screen) to fill the screen with the patient's text; a small control strip stays at the edge with the main actions. Esc returns.

Next step / Previous step move through the test; Save and finish saves the test and opens it on the Results page.

Symptoms

The patient looks at the passage of text (on white for the baseline, on the chosen colour afterwards). Text, font, size, line spacing and number of rows can be changed beside the questions; choose settings that bring out any visual stress symptoms.

Having allowed adequate time for symptoms to develop, ask the four questions and grade each from 0 (none) to 5 (severe):

  1. Do the words appear to move, wobble or flicker?
  2. Do the words look too close together?
  3. Does the page look too bright or dazzling?
  4. Does it hurt your eyes to look at the page?

Keys: 0 – 5 grade the highlighted question, Up/Down move between questions, Enter goes on. Other symptoms can be typed in the notes.

Rate of reading

The Rate of Reading test, designed by Prof Wilkins, is a passage of very common words in random order that the patient reads aloud as quickly and accurately as possible. The small, closely spaced text maximises any visual difficulty.

There is often a practice effect, so let the patient read for about a minute first. Then start the clock (Space or Start clock); it stops by itself after the reading time set in Settings > Test setup (a sound can be played).

Marking

On the textMarks
Click a wordA misread word (error). Click again to remove.

The counts can also be typed. Reading speed = (words read − 15 × rows skipped) × 60 / seconds, in words per minute.

Determination of the preferred colour

Automatic

ReadEZ compares the colours in pairs and the patient says which is more comfortable. Three interleaved staircases (starting from colours A, E and H) converge on the preferred colour in about 33 presentations; the leading colours are then compared with each other and the winner with grey. For lenses and computer the light and dark tints are also compared.

  • Overlays: two passages side by side. The patient says left or right (keys ←/→).
  • Lenses / Computer: the whole screen shows colour 1, then colour 2 (presentation time in Settings, adjustable during the test), then white. The patient says first or second (←/→); R repeats the pair.

The colour wheel shows how often each colour was chosen: a single long spoke means a clear preference; many short spokes mean the patient was inconsistent.

Manual

Choose the colours yourself from the colour wheels (outer ring: light tints, inner ring: dark tints) and select the preferred one. Useful for a quick assessment or when the patient cannot complete the automatic test. A bespoke colour can also be chosen.

Results

The list shows the patient's tests. Select one to see:

  • Colour chosen: the colour, its set and letter, and the method.
  • Change with colour: reading speed, errors and symptoms compared with the latest baseline.
  • Symptoms chart and rate of reading (with the transcript of the reading).

Show on patient's screen shows the text in the chosen colour (useful when ordering an overlay or lens). See also Reports and letters.

Reports and letters

Report prints the patient's details, the colour chosen, the change with colour, symptoms and reading. Letter writes to a parent/carer or a teacher.

The preview can print, save as PDF or Word, and e-mail. Your practice name and logo (Settings > General) appear at the top.

Letter templates

The letter's paragraphs are text files in Documents\ReadEZ Data\ReadEZ Templates: ParentOpeningPara.txt, ParentClosingPara.txt, TeacherOpeningPara.txt and TeacherClosingPara.txt. Edit them with Notepad. These fields are filled in: {Firstname}, {Surname}, {DateOfTest} and {Result} (for example “overlay in colour F”).
